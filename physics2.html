Section 01

Introduction to Motion

NCERT §2.1 — What is motion & types of motion

If an object changes its position with respect to time relative to a reference point, it is said to be in motion. Motion is relative — the same object can be at rest for one observer and in motion for another.

📍 Reference Point / Origin
A fixed point with respect to which position is measured. Usually denoted as O. The reference frame includes the origin and a coordinate system.
✅ Point Object
  • Size is negligible compared to distance traveled
  • Eg: Earth in solar system (treated as point)
  • Train between two distant stations
  • Monkey on a circular track (vs track size)
❌ NOT a Point Object
  • Cricket ball turning sharply (size ≈ turn distance)
  • Beaker tumbling off a table
  • Earth during rotation around its axis
💡
NCERT Tip: Always check whether the object's size is negligible compared to the scale of motion before treating it as a point object. This is a common MCQ trap!

Section 02

Position, Path Length & Displacement

NCERT §2.2 — The most fundamental concepts
📌 Position (x)
Location of an object on a number line (x-axis) at any given time t. It can be positive, negative, or zero depending on direction from origin.
ConceptPath Length (Distance)Displacement
NatureScalar (no direction)Vector (has direction)
DefinitionTotal length of path traveledShortest distance from start to end point
Symbols or dΔx = x₂ − x₁
ValueAlways ≥ 0 (can't be negative)Can be +ve, −ve, or zero
ExampleWalk 3 km North, then 4 km East → s = 7 kmSame journey → |Δx| = 5 km (NE direction)
🏠
🌍 Real Life Example — Going to School
You walk from home to school via a curved road — the path length (distance) is whatever the odometer shows: say 2.5 km. But the displacement is a straight line from your home to school: maybe only 1.8 km (in a specific direction). If you come back home after school, total distance = 5 km, but displacement = 0!
⚠️
Common Mistake: Many students confuse "displacement = 0" with "no motion". If you walk in a circle and return to start → displacement = 0, but distance ≠ 0. This is a classic CBSE 1-mark trick question!
Displacement Formula
Δx = x₂ − x₁ = xfinal − xinitial
Can be positive (forward), negative (backward), or zero. Magnitude of displacement ≤ Path length

Section 03

Speed & Velocity

NCERT §2.3 — Average, Uniform & Instantaneous
PropertySpeedVelocity
TypeScalarVector
FormulaSpeed = Distance / Timev = Displacement / Time
SI Unitm/sm/s (with direction)
ValueAlways ≥ 0Can be +ve, −ve, zero
ExampleSpeedometer reads 60 km/h60 km/h towards North
Average Speed
v̄ = Total Path Length / Total Time
Always positive scalar
Average Velocity
v̄ = Δx / Δt = (x₂ − x₁) / (t₂ − t₁)
Can be +ve, −ve, or zero
⚡ Instantaneous Velocity
Velocity at a particular instant of time. Mathematically it is the derivative of position with respect to time:

v = lim(Δt→0) Δx/Δt = dx/dt

On an x–t graph, instantaneous velocity = slope of tangent at that point.
🚗
Real Life — Delhi to Agra Road Trip
Delhi to Agra is 206 km by highway (distance) but ~180 km in a straight line (displacement). If you take 3 hours: Average speed = 206/3 ≈ 68.7 km/h, but Average velocity = 180/3 = 60 km/h (towards Agra). Your speedometer shows instantaneous speed at each moment.
A woman walks 2.5 km to her office in 30 min, stays till 5 PM, then returns by auto in 6 min. Find average speed and average velocity for whole journey. (NCERT Example)
1Total distance = 2.5 + 2.5 = 5 km = 5000 m
2Total displacement = 0 m (returned to start)
3Total time = 30 min + 8h 30min + 6 min = 9.1 hours (walking + stay + return)
4For the walking and return ONLY: time = 36 min = 0.6 h
✅ Avg speed (travel only) = 5 km / 0.6 h ≈ 8.33 km/h  |  Avg velocity (whole day) = 0 (net displacement = 0)
💡
HC Verma Insight: If a particle moves with speed v₁ for first half distance and v₂ for second half distance, then average speed = 2v₁v₂/(v₁+v₂) — the harmonic mean! (Most repeated JEE formula)

Section 04

Acceleration

NCERT §2.4 — Rate of change of velocity
📐 Acceleration (a)
Rate of change of velocity with time. It is a vector quantity. SI unit: m/s²

Average acceleration = Δv / Δt = (v₂ − v₁) / (t₂ − t₁)
Instantaneous acceleration = dv/dt = d²x/dt²
Positive Acceleration
  • Velocity increasing in +ve direction
  • OR velocity decreasing in −ve direction
  • Car speeding up while moving forward
Negative Acceleration (Retardation)
  • Velocity decreasing in +ve direction
  • OR velocity increasing in −ve direction
  • Car braking — decelerating
✈️
Real Life — Airplane Takeoff & Landing
During takeoff, the aircraft accelerates from 0 to ~280 km/h in about 30 seconds — that's roughly +2.6 m/s² of acceleration. During landing, it decelerates (negative acceleration / retardation) from ~250 km/h to 0. You feel yourself pushed back during acceleration and pressed forward during braking — that's Newton's laws in action!
⚠️
Key Insight: A body can have zero velocity but non-zero acceleration! Example: A ball thrown upward — at the highest point, v = 0 but a = −g = −9.8 m/s² (still acting downward). This is one of the most common misconceptions students have!

Section 05

Kinematic Equations

NCERT §2.5 — For Uniformly Accelerated Motion (UAM)
📌
Valid ONLY when: acceleration a = constant (uniform acceleration). These 3 equations connect u (initial velocity), v (final velocity), a (acceleration), s (displacement), t (time).
Equation 1 — Velocity-Time
v = u + at
Use when: s is unknown
Equation 2 — Displacement-Time
s = ut + ½at²
Use when: v is unknown
Equation 3 — Velocity-Displacement
v² = u² + 2as
Use when: t is unknown
Bonus — nth Second
sn = u + a(2n−1)/2
Distance in nth second of motion (JEE FAVOURITE!)
Average Velocity in UAM
v̄ = (u + v) / 2
Valid ONLY for uniform acceleration. Very useful shortcut!
🏏
Real Life — Sixes in Cricket
When MS Dhoni smashes a six, the ball leaves the bat with initial velocity u ≈ 36 m/s upward. Once in air, only gravity acts: a = −10 m/s². Using v² = u² + 2as, maximum height = u²/2g = (36)²/20 = 64.8 m! That's the height of a 20-storey building — and it comes down using the same equations.
A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 5 m/s². Find: (a) velocity after 4s, (b) distance covered in 4s, (c) distance in 4th second. (NCERT type)
GGiven: u = 0, a = 5 m/s², t = 4s
av = u + at = 0 + 5×4 = 20 m/s
bs = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½×5×16 = 40 m
cs₄ = u + a(2n−1)/2 = 0 + 5×(7)/2 = 17.5 m
✅ v = 20 m/s  |  s = 40 m  |  s₄th = 17.5 m
🧠
Memory Trick — "SUVat": S= displacement, U= initial velocity, V= final velocity, A= acceleration, T= time. Every kinematic equation uses exactly 4 of these 5 quantities — find the one missing and choose the right equation!

Section 06

Graphs of Motion

NCERT §2.6 — x-t, v-t, a-t graphs — most asked in JEE!

Graphical analysis is the most visual and powerful tool for understanding motion. Master the 3 key graphs and you can solve any kinematics problem without equations!

📈 Position–Time (x–t) Graphs
Object at Rest
Horizontal line: x = constant, v = slope = 0
Uniform Velocity
Straight inclined line: slope = v = constant
Uniformly Accelerated
Parabolic curve: slope increases → v increases
📐
Key Rule: On an x–t graph, slope = instantaneous velocity. Steeper slope → greater speed. Negative slope → moving backward.
📉 Velocity–Time (v–t) Graphs
Zero Acceleration
Horizontal: v=const, a=slope=0. Area = displacement
Uniform Acceleration
Straight incline: slope = a. Area under = displacement!
Retardation then Acceleration
V-shape: velocity decreases, becomes 0, increases again
⭐ GOLDEN RULES FOR v-t GRAPHS
  • Slope of v-t graph = Acceleration (a = Δv/Δt)
  • Area under v-t graph = Displacement (area below x-axis = negative displacement)
  • If v-t graph is a straight line → uniform acceleration (UAM applies)
  • If the line crosses the time axis → object momentarily at rest and then reverses direction
  • Steep positive slope → large acceleration; steep negative slope → large retardation
🚇
Real Life — Delhi Metro Ride
A Metro train accelerates from rest at Rajiv Chowk, reaches a constant speed between stations (horizontal v-t line), then decelerates smoothly to stop at Patel Chowk. The v-t graph looks like a trapezoid! The area of this trapezoid = total distance between stations. This is exactly how metro engineers calculate travel times!

Section 07

Free Fall & Vertical Motion Under Gravity

NCERT §2.7 — Most important for JEE & CBSE numericals
🍎 Free Fall
Motion of an object under the influence of gravity only (no air resistance). ALL objects fall with the same acceleration g = 9.8 m/s² ≈ 10 m/s² regardless of mass — proven by Galileo by dropping objects from Leaning Tower of Pisa!
Free Fall Equations (taking downward as +ve)
v = u + gt  |  h = ut + ½gt²  |  v² = u² + 2gh
g = 9.8 m/s² (use 10 m/s² in JEE for easy calculation)  |  for upward motion, use a = −g
🔽 Object Dropped (u=0)
  • Initial velocity = 0
  • v = gt   (velocity increases linearly)
  • h = ½gt²   (parabolic fall)
  • v = √(2gh)
  • Time to hit ground: t = √(2H/g)
🔼 Object Thrown Upward (u > 0)
  • a = −g (decelerating going up)
  • At max height: v = 0
  • Max height H = u²/2g
  • Time to reach top: t = u/g
  • Total time in air: T = 2u/g
  • Returns with same speed u (no air resist.)
⭐ JEE Special — Distances in Successive Seconds

For a body dropped from rest, distances in 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th... seconds are in the ratio:

Galileo's Odd Number Law
1 : 3 : 5 : 7 : 9 ... (odd numbers)
Distance in nth second from rest: s_n = g(2n−1)/2  |  Total distance after n seconds: g·n²/2
🏗️
Real Life — Dropping a Brick from a Building
A brick dropped from a 20m building: time to reach ground = √(2×20/10) = 2 seconds. It hits at v = gt = 10×2 = 20 m/s ≈ 72 km/h! This is why construction sites use safety nets. In the 1st second it falls 5m, in the 2nd second it falls 15m — a 3× ratio (odd number law at work).
A ball is thrown vertically upward from a tower of height 25 m with initial velocity 20 m/s. Find: (a) max height from ground, (b) time to hit ground. (g = 10 m/s²) — Classic JEE Type
1Max height above throw point: H = u²/2g = 400/20 = 20 m
2Max height from ground = 25 + 20 = 45 m
3For total fall: take upward as +ve, displacement = −25m
4−25 = 20t − ½×10×t² → 5t² − 20t − 25 = 0 → t² − 4t − 5 = 0
5(t−5)(t+1) = 0 → t = 5s (taking positive value)
✅ Max height from ground = 45 m  |  Time to hit ground = 5 seconds

Section 08

Relative Velocity

NCERT §2.7 — How fast is one object moving w.r.t. another?
🔄 Relative Velocity
Velocity of object A as observed from object B. It depends on the velocities of both objects and their directions of motion.
Relative Velocity Formula
vAB = vA − vB
vAB = velocity of A with respect to B  |  vBA = vB − vA = −vAB
Same Direction (→→)

vAB = vA − vB

If both move at same speed → relative velocity = 0 (they appear stationary to each other)

Opposite Direction (→←)

vAB = vA + vB

Relative speed is maximum when objects move towards each other

🚂
Real Life — Two Trains
Train A moves at 72 km/h, Train B at 54 km/h. Same direction: relative speed = 72−54 = 18 km/h (A seems to crawl past B). Opposite direction: relative speed = 72+54 = 126 km/h (they zoom past each other quickly). That's why head-on collisions are so much more devastating — the impact speed is the sum of both speeds!
A police jeep at 54 km/h chases a thief at 90 km/h in the same direction. The police fires a bullet at 180 m/s. What is the speed of bullet relative to the thief? (JEE & HC Verma type)
1Convert: Police = 54 km/h = 15 m/s, Thief = 90 km/h = 25 m/s
2Bullet velocity w.r.t. ground = 180 + 15 = 195 m/s (bullet fired forward from moving jeep)
3Bullet velocity w.r.t. thief = 195 − 25 = 170 m/s
✅ Speed of bullet relative to thief = 170 m/s
💡
NCERT Classic: If you are on a train moving at 100 km/h and throw a ball forward at 10 km/h relative to train, the ball moves at 110 km/h relative to ground. This is how relative velocity adds up!

PYQs

Previous Year Questions

Most repeated questions from CBSE Board & JEE Main 2018–2024
📝 CBSE Board Exam — Most Repeated Questions
CBSE20243 Marks
Draw position-time graph for (a) a body at rest, (b) a body moving with uniform velocity, (c) a body moving with uniform acceleration. Explain the significance of slope of each graph.
✅ Slope of x-t graph = instantaneous velocity. (a) horizontal line v=0, (b) straight inclined line v=constant, (c) parabolic curve, slope increases = accelerating
CBSE20232 Marks
Can a body have zero velocity and still be accelerating? Give one example.
✅ YES — A ball thrown vertically upward has v=0 at maximum height but acceleration = g = 9.8 m/s² downward throughout its flight.
CBSE20225 Marks
Derive the three equations of motion for uniformly accelerated motion graphically using a v-t graph.
✅ Draw v-t graph with u at t=0 and v at time t. Use slope = a for eq.1. Area of trapezoid = displacement for eq.2. Eliminate t for eq.3. This derivation comes EVERY YEAR!
CBSE20223 Marks
A ball is dropped from a height of 19.6 m. Find: (a) time to reach ground, (b) velocity with which it hits the ground. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
✅ (a) h = ½gt² → t = √(2×19.6/9.8) = 2 s  |  (b) v = gt = 9.8×2 = 19.6 m/s
CBSE20212 Marks
Distinguish between distance and displacement with suitable examples. Can displacement be greater than distance?
✅ Displacement ≤ Distance always. Distance = total path length (scalar); Displacement = shortest straight line between start and end points (vector). Displacement can NEVER exceed distance.
CBSE20203 Marks
The velocity-time graph of a particle is given. Find the displacement in 0–8 s and acceleration during 4–8 s. (v goes 0→20 m/s in 4s then stays 20 m/s till 8s)
✅ Displacement = area of trapezoid = ½(4)(20) + 20(4) = 40 + 80 = 120 m  |  Acceleration (4-8s) = 0 (constant velocity)
CBSE20192 Marks
What does the area under acceleration-time (a-t) graph represent?
✅ Area under a-t graph = Change in velocity (Δv = v − u). This is often forgotten — remember: area under v-t = displacement, area under a-t = velocity change!
🎯 JEE Main — Most Repeated Questions
JEE MainApril 2024 Shift I4 Marks
A body travels 102.5 m in nth second and 115 m in (n+2)th second. Find the acceleration.
✅ s_n = u + a(2n−1)/2 and s_(n+2) = u + a(2n+3)/2. Subtracting: 115−102.5 = 2a → a = 12.5/2 = 6.25 m/s²
JEE MainJan 2024 Shift II4 Marks
Train A moves North at 72 km/h, Train B moves South at 108 km/h. Find velocity of B w.r.t. A and velocity of ground w.r.t. B.
✅ v_A = +20 m/s, v_B = −30 m/s (southward = negative). v_BA = v_B − v_A = −30−20 = −50 m/s (south). v_ground w.r.t. B = 0 − (−30) = +30 m/s (north)
JEE MainApr 2023 Shift I4 Marks
Position of a particle: x = (5t² − 4t + 5) m. Find magnitude of velocity at t = 2s.
✅ v = dx/dt = 10t − 4. At t=2: v = 20−4 = 16 m/s
JEE MainAug 2021 Shift I4 Marks
Two balls thrown upward with same initial velocity 35 m/s, 3 seconds apart. At what height do they collide? (g = 10 m/s²)
✅ Let 2nd ball be at time t, 1st at (t+3). Setting heights equal: 35t − 5t² = 35(t+3) − 5(t+3)². Solving → t = 4s. Height = 35(4) − 5(16) = 140−80 = 60 m
JEE MainJan 2020 Shift II4 Marks
Ball dropped from 100m high tower on a planet. In last ½s before hitting ground, it covers 19m. Find g on that planet.
✅ Total time T: 100 = ½gT². At (T−0.5)s: h = ½g(T−0.5)². Covered in last ½s = 100 − ½g(T−0.5)² = 19. Also gT = v. Solving simultaneously → g = 8 m/s²
JEE MainFeb 2021 Morning4 Marks
If velocity–time graph has shape AMB (increases, constant, decreases), what would the corresponding acceleration-time graph look like?
✅ a-t graph would show: (A to M) = positive constant value; (at M) = drop to zero; (M to B) = negative constant value — a step function pattern

🧠 10 Golden Rules to Never Forget
  • Displacement ≤ Distance always. Equal only for straight-line motion in one direction.
  • Avg velocity = 0 doesn't mean no motion — it means net displacement = 0 (returned to start).
  • Slope of x-t = velocity; Slope of v-t = acceleration; Area under v-t = displacement
  • At max height of vertical throw: v=0 but a = g ≠ 0 — most common wrong answer!
  • 3 equations of motion valid ONLY for constant acceleration (uniform acceleration).
  • For 2 equal time intervals: avg velocity = (v₁+v₂)/2. For 2 equal distances: avg speed = 2v₁v₂/(v₁+v₂).
  • Free fall: all objects fall with same acceleration g — mass doesn't matter (no air resistance).
  • Objects thrown upward and dropped take same time if they reach same point — symmetric motion.
  • Odd number law: distances fallen in 1st, 2nd, 3rd second from rest = 1:3:5 (g/2, 3g/2, 5g/2)
  • Relative velocity same direction: subtract. Opposite direction: add velocities.
